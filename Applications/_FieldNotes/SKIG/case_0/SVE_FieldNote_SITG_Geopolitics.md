## 1. Context

Applied Taleb's "Skin in the Game" (SITG) framework to evaluate asymmetry between decision-making authority and personal risk exposure in geopolitical conflict escalation. The core hypothesis: decision-makers with maximum authority over war consequences face minimum personal exposure to those consequences, creating a principal-agent problem with catastrophic stakes.

**Why this matters:** If those who decide war bear no cost, incentive structures become misaligned with civilian welfare. This is not merely philosophical—it predicts escalation bias and extended conflicts.

---

## 2. Claims Evaluated

**Primary Hypothesis:**  
*"Inverted Symmetry Law"* — Decision-making power correlates inversely with personal risk exposure in conflict scenarios.

**Specific Sub-Claims:**
1. Western political leaders show high insulation (secure capitals, no family draft risk)
2. Defense industry executives exhibit maximum asymmetry (profit from conflict, zero personal risk)
3. Leaders in active war zones show aligned incentives (Zelensky case)
4. This asymmetry is systematic, not random

---

## 3. Protocols Used

**Primary:** Multi-Evaluator Comparative Framework (MECF)  
**Components:**
- Socratic decomposition of decision-maker exposure
- Tier-based comparative analysis (avoiding false precision)
- Multi-AI consensus methodology (independent evaluation by 5 AI systems)
- Cross-cultural validation (human evaluation across 5 geographic regions, N=444 from related work)

**Secondary:** EBP principles (Epistemological Boxing Protocol)  
- Explicitly labeled tier assignments as descriptive, not measured
- Separated facts (verifiable data) from assessments (comparative judgments)
- Documented methodology transparently

## 5. What Happened

### Consensus Findings Across All AI Evaluators:

**1. Inverted Relationship Confirmed (Qualitatively)**
- All 5 AI systems independently identified inverse pattern
- High decision-making power → Low personal risk (consistent across evaluators)
- Pattern held across different scoring methodologies

**2. Tier Convergence:**

**Tier 1 (Maximum Exposure):**  
- Zelensky: All AIs rated highest personal risk
- Key markers: Remained in Kyiv under bombardment, multiple assassination attempts, family at risk
- Claude: "Maximum Personal Exposure (Aligned Incentives)"
- Gemini: SITG=95, Kids=80
- Grok: SITG=90, Kids=80

**Tier 2 (Moderate Insulation):**  
- Putin: Physical insulation (bunkers) but political survival tied to war
- Merz: Low personal risk but some military background
- Netanyahu: Combat background but current high insulation

**Tier 3 (High Insulation):**  
- Western leaders (Macron, Scholz, von der Leyen, Merkel)
- No physical exposure, no children in military, secure capitals
- Gemini: SITG range 5-10 across this group
- Grok: SITG range 5-15

**Tier 4 (Maximum Asymmetry - Defense Industry):**  
- ALL AIs placed defense CEOs in lowest personal risk category
- Taiclet (Lockheed): $23.75M compensation (2024), 189× median employee pay
- Papperger (Rheinmetall): Assassination target (foiled 2024) — ONLY case of CEO facing kinetic risk
- Woodburn (BAE): Stock rose 143% during conflict period

**3. Economic Incentive Inversion:**  
- Defense industry shows *positive* correlation: profit ↑ when conflict ↑
- K2/Kimi explicitly mapped this as "convexity" problem
- Gemini labeled as "Profiteer / Escalator" role

**4. The Zelensky Exception:**  
- All evaluators identified this as the ONLY case of aligned incentives at high authority
- Claude: "rare case where decision-making authority is paired with maximum personal exposure"
- K2: "90% SITG vs. 0-20% for Western decision-makers"

### Inter-Rater Agreement:
- **Pattern detection:** 5/5 AIs identified inverted relationship
- **Tier assignment:** High agreement on extremes (Zelensky vs. Defense CEOs)
- **Numerical scores:** Varied (Gemini/Grok used 0-100, Claude used descriptive tiers, K2 used asymmetry index)
- **Interpretation:** Consensus on moral hazard implication

## 6. What Failed or Surprised

### Failures:
1. **Initial Claude Refusal:** Claude refused the first prompt, citing concerns about "speculative personal data" and "false precision" — had to reframe as comparative analysis
2. **Numerical Precision Illusion:** Some AIs (Gemini, Grok) generated precise scores (e.g., "SITG=47") that imply measurement but are actually comparative estimates
3. **Missing Data:** For some figures (e.g., Blinken, Stoltenberg), limited information led to "insufficient data" or lower confidence ratings
4. **Verification Gap:** Did not systematically verify AI-generated factual claims (e.g., compensation figures, family details)

### Surprises:
1. **Papperger Anomaly:** Only defense CEO with personal risk (assassination target) — creates partial incentive alignment
2. **Cross-Model Stability:** Despite different methodologies, core pattern replicated across all 5 systems
3. **K2 Sophistication:** Kimi/K2 provided most rigorous theoretical framing (Asymmetry Quadrant, Lyapunov dynamics analogy)
4. **The "Conscription Shield":** Western leaders benefit from suspended conscription — structural insulation from family-level consequences

## 7. What Was Revised

### Methodological Clarifications:
1. **Precision Claims:** Shifted from "quantified" to "comparative framework-based analysis"
2. **Data Type:** Explicitly labeled as "tier-based descriptive analysis" not "statistical measurement"
3. **AI Role:** Positioned as "multi-evaluator consensus" not "objective measurement"
4. **Uncertainty:** Acknowledged that AI assessments are informed estimates, not verified facts

### Conceptual Refinements:
1. **Added "Feedback Loop" dimension:** How quickly decisions affect the decision-maker
2. **Distinguished:** Economic alignment (general interests) vs. Economic convexity (profit from volatility)
3. **Identified:** Temporal distance as key variable (immediate consequences vs. delayed electoral feedback)

## 11. Adversarial Questions to Address

**Q1: "Aren't AI assessments just reflecting training data bias?"**  
A: Yes, partially. But convergence across 5 independent systems (different training sets, different companies) provides some validation. This is similar to inter-rater reliability in qualitative research.

**Q2: "Can you prove causation?"**  
A: No. This is a descriptive/comparative analysis, not a causal study. We document pattern, not mechanism.

**Q3: "Why not use real data?"**  
A: Time constraint + data access limitations. This is a framework demonstration. Full quantification requires dedicated research project.

**Q4: "Is this just confirming your priors?"**  
A: Possible. Mitigation: Used multiple independent evaluators, included falsification attempts (looked for counter-examples), documented surprises (Papperger case).
