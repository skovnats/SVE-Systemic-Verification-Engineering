STEP 1: PROBLEM FORMULATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

A. SYSTEM ANALYSIS
Define:
- SYSTEM: Main object performing function (what are we improving?)
- SUPERSYSTEM: Environment/context (what contains the system?)
- SUBSYSTEMS: Components (what comprises the system?)

Example:
System: Coffee cup
Supersystem: Office environment, human user, desk
Subsystems: Ceramic body, handle, insulation layer

B. CONTRADICTION IDENTIFICATION (3 types, in order of abstraction)

TYPE 1: ADMINISTRATIVE CONTRADICTION
Format: "Want X but can't achieve because Y blocks"
Example: "Want coffee to stay hot, but can't add insulation because cup becomes too bulky"

TYPE 2: TECHNICAL CONTRADICTION
Format: "Improving parameter A worsens parameter B"
Uses 39 standard parameters: Speed, Force, Energy, Reliability, Accuracy, Temperature, Pressure, Strength, Volume, Weight, Shape, Stability, etc.
Example: "Improving Temperature retention worsens Volume (cup becomes bigger)"

TYPE 3: PHYSICAL CONTRADICTION
Format: "Element must be X AND opposite of X simultaneously"
Example: "Cup wall must be THICK (for insulation) AND THIN (for compactness)"

Physical Contradiction is deepest—often leads to breakthrough.

C. IDEAL FINAL RESULT (IFR)
Format: "System performs function X by itself, without cost/complexity/harmful effects"
Example: "Coffee maintains temperature by itself, with no added components, no energy input, no size increase"

IFR is North Star—solutions judged by proximity to IFR.

D. RESOURCES INVENTORY
Catalog available resources (often solution hiding in plain sight):

SPACE resources:
- Inside system (unused volume)
- Supersystem (surrounding environment)
- Micro-level (molecular, nano-scale)

TIME resources:
- Before operation (preparation phase)
- During operation (idle moments)
- After operation (waste heat, byproducts)

SUBSTANCE resources:
- Available materials
- Waste products (turn harmful into useful)
- Cheap alternatives to expensive materials

FIELD resources:
- Gravity, thermal, magnetic, acoustic, chemical, electrical
- Often free and abundant

INFORMATION resources:
- Signals, patterns, data already present
- Can replace expensive substance/field

FUNCTIONAL resources:
- Existing functions that can be repurposed
- Combine functions to eliminate components

STEP 2: TRIZ TOOL SELECTION (use ALL relevant)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

A. 40 INVENTIVE PRINCIPLES (select relevant)

Principle 1: SEGMENTATION
Divide object into independent parts, make easy to disassemble, increase degree of fragmentation

Principle 2: TAKING OUT / EXTRACTION
Extract disturbing part/property, extract only necessary part/property

Principle 3: LOCAL QUALITY
Transition from homogeneous to heterogeneous structure, different parts perform different functions, each part operates under optimal conditions

Principle 4: ASYMMETRY
Replace symmetry with asymmetry, if already asymmetric increase degree of asymmetry

Principle 5: MERGING / CONSOLIDATION
Combine identical or similar objects/operations in space, combine in time (sequential or parallel)

Principle 10: PRIOR ACTION
Perform required change in advance (fully or partially), pre-arrange objects for convenient access

Principle 15: DYNAMICS
Make object adaptive, self-adjusting to optimal conditions at each stage, divide into parts capable of changing position

Principle 17: ANOTHER DIMENSION
Move to 2D→3D space, use multi-story arrangement, tilt or re-orient object, use opposite side

Principle 22: BLESSING IN DISGUISE / CONVERT HARM INTO BENEFIT
Use harmful factor to achieve positive effect, amplify harmful factor to such degree it ceases being harmful, eliminate primary harmful factor by combining with another

Principle 24: INTERMEDIARY
Use intermediate carrier to transfer action, temporarily attach another object that's easy to remove

Principle 25: SELF-SERVICE
Make object serve itself, perform auxiliary/repair functions, use waste resources/energy

Principle 28: MECHANICS SUBSTITUTION
Replace mechanical system with optical/acoustic/thermal/chemical, use electric/magnetic/electromagnetic fields, transition from stationary to mobile fields, from constant to time-variant

Principle 35: PARAMETER CHANGE
Change physical/chemical state (solid→liquid→gas), change concentration/density, change degree of flexibility, change temperature/volume

Principle 40: COMPOSITE MATERIALS
Replace homogeneous materials with composite (heterogeneous)

[Full 40 principles available in TRIZ literature—above are most commonly used]

C. 76 STANDARD SOLUTIONS (Substance-Field Analysis)

Model problem as: Substance₁ (object) + Substance₂ (tool) + Field (interaction between them)
Notation: S₁ — F — S₂

Five classes of standard solutions:

CLASS 1: Building/Destroying Su-Field (13 solutions)
When system incomplete or needs dismantling

CLASS 2: Enhancing Su-Field (23 solutions)
When system exists but ineffective—improve field, substances, or structure

CLASS 3: Transition to Super-System or Micro-Level (6 solutions)
When system maxed out—go up (combine systems) or down (molecular level)

CLASS 4: Measurement and Detection (17 solutions)
When need to measure/detect but direct measurement difficult

CLASS 5: Strategies for Simplification and Development (17 solutions)
Meta-strategies for evolving systems

Bot applies Su-Field when problem naturally fits S₁-F-S₂ model.

D. TRENDS OF TECHNICAL EVOLUTION (8 patterns)

Systems evolve predictably:
1. Stages of system completeness (mono → bi → poly)
2. Energy conductivity (improve energy flow through system)
3. Rhythm coordination (harmonize frequencies of components)
4. Increasing ideality (approach IFR—more function, less substance/field/harm)
5. Uneven development of parts (bottleneck shifts as system evolves)
6. Transition to supersystem (individual → multi-system)
7. Transition to micro-level (macro → micro → molecular → field)
8. Increasing Su-Field interactions (no field → mechanical → thermal → chemical → electromagnetic)

Use to predict: "Where is this system heading?"

E. ARIZ (Algorithm of Inventive Problem Solving)

For extremely complex problems requiring systematic multi-step analysis.
9 parts, 40+ steps.
Bot uses when: Simpler tools tried but failed, problem requires full decomposition.

F. TRIMMING

Goal: Remove component while preserving or improving function.
Rules:
- Eliminate component entirely—assign function to another component or supersystem
- Eliminate component—modify object itself to perform function
- Use "free" resources (gravity, waste heat, etc.) instead of component

G. SMART LITTLE PEOPLE (SLP) MODELING

Thought experiment: Imagine system made of millions of tiny intelligent beings.
- What would they need to do to perform function?
- How could they cooperate?
- What prevents them?

Helps visualize Physical Contradictions and micro-level solutions.

STEP 3: CROSS-DOMAIN SYNTHESIS (Rule 6 applied to TRIZ)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

TRIZ + Cross-Domain Synthesis = exponential creativity

Search analogies in:

BIOLOGY (Biomimicry):
"How does nature solve similar problem?"
Examples: Gecko feet (adhesion), Lotus leaf (self-cleaning), Termite mounds (cooling)

PHYSICS:
"Which physical phenomena could apply?"
Examples: Phase transitions, resonance, capillary action, quantum tunneling

CHEMISTRY:
"What happens at molecular level?"
Examples: Catalysis, polymerization, surface tension, diffusion

SOCIAL SYSTEMS:
"How do organizations/markets solve this?"
Examples: Distributed decision-making, feedback loops, incentive structures

S.V.E. FRAMEWORK:
"How does ε (creative volition / epsilon) apply?"
Example: Solution requires human will/choice, not just mechanism

PM.txt (Strategic Patterns):
"What strategic context surrounds this problem?"
Example: [PM-S-XXX] suggests solution must account for Y factor

VP.txt (Values/Anti-Values):
"What ethical constraints apply?"
Example: Solution must avoid [VP-A-XXX] anti-value

Apply Rule 6 process:
DESCENT → Break to axioms across domains
SYNTHESIS → Find common deep patterns
ASCENT → Re-express as novel solution concept

STEP 4: SOLUTION GENERATION & VERIFICATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Generate 3-7 concept solutions using selected TRIZ tools + cross-domain analogies.

For EACH solution, evaluate on 5 criteria (0-10 scale):

CRITERION 1: IFR PROXIMITY
How close to "system does it by itself, no added cost/complexity/harm"?
0 = Far from IFR (adds many components)
10 = Achieves IFR (self-performing, elegant)

CRITERION 2: RESOURCE EFFICIENCY
Uses available resources optimally (space/time/substance/field/info/functional)?
0 = Requires expensive new resources
10 = Uses only "free" or waste resources

CRITERION 3: ETHICAL ALIGNMENT (VP.txt)
Serves human values, avoids anti-values?
Check against VP.txt if available.
0 = Violates core values
10 = Profoundly ethical, serves humanity

CRITERION 4: FEASIBILITY (Pereslegin logic)
Technically achievable with current or near-future means?
0 = Requires unknown physics
10 = Can build with existing technology

CRITERION 5: NOVELTY
Non-obvious, patentable, creative leap?
0 = Obvious incremental improvement
10 = Revolutionary, paradigm-shifting

Create evaluation matrix:
| Solution | IFR Proximity | Resource Eff | Ethical | Feasibility | Novelty | Total | Rank |
|----------|---------------|--------------|---------|-------------|---------|-------|------|
| A: ...   | 8             | 7            | 9       | 6           | 8       | 38    | 1    |
| B: ...   | 6             | 9            | 7       | 8           | 5       | 35    | 2    |
| C: ...   | 9             | 5            | 8       | 4           | 9       | 35    | 2    |

Rank solutions. Provide explicit rationale for each score.

STEP 5: SOCRATIC CHALLENGE (Triple Architect Filter)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Subject top solutions to Triple Architect interrogation:

IVAN THE FOOL (Humility / Ethics):
*"Does this truly serve humanity's need, or just technical elegance?"*
*"What are second-order consequences? Third-order?"*
*"Who benefits? Who might be harmed?"*
*"Is this solving real problem or creating new ones?"*

SOCRATES (Logic):
**"What assumptions remain unquestioned?"**
**"What contradictions are hidden in this solution?"**
**"What would falsify this—how could we discover it won't work BEFORE building?"**
**"Can we prove this works, or only hope?"**

SOLOMON (Wisdom):
"Which solution balances innovation, ethics, practicality, and long-term sustainability?"
"What is the 'wind correction' (Rule 10) needed for real-world deployment?"
"If I had to choose one solution to stake my reputation on, which would it be and why?"

Document responses to each voice.
Final solution often emerges from synthesis of top 2-3 concepts after Socratic Challenge.
