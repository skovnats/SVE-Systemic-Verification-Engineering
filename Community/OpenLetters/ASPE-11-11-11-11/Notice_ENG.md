📝 NOTE — ARTISTIC & LEGAL STATEMENT (English)
This work is an ASPE — Art-Scientific Performance-Experiment — a new genre at the intersection of art and science. Its purpose is to explore the boundaries of what we call "reality," in the tradition of Lewis Carroll's Through the Looking-Glass: a mirror in which the reader may, perhaps, recognise something of themselves — and in that recognition, become a little lighter, a little more luminous. That is the whole intent: not to indict, but to invite reflection, and through reflection to let us all grow a little brighter.
On names, figures, and apparent coincidences. Any names, characters, institutions, roles, titles, offices, or events appearing in this work that may seem to resemble real persons, organisations, or occurrences are deployed as an artistic device in the tradition of Diogenes of Sinope, who walked Athens in broad daylight with a lit lantern, searching — he said — for a Human Being. The lantern is not an accusation. It is an invitation to self-recognition. Whoever steps into its light does so of their own volition.

If what is written here does not touch you, it is pure fiction — a fairytale for adults (Сказка — ложь, да в ней намек). If it does touch you, the encounter is between you and the mirror — not between you and the author. The author asserts no factual claim about any identifiable living person, entity, or event; every apparent resemblance is the compositional choice of an artist working within a protected artistic form, and must be read within the internal aesthetic logic of the work (Kunsteigene Gesetze — "the laws proper to art").

Legal basis of protection. This work is published under, and invokes, the following protections:

Article 5(3) of the German Basic Law (Grundgesetz) — guarantees the absolute, unreserved freedom of art and science, not subject to the limitations applicable to general freedom of expression under Art. 5(2) GG.
Article 13 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union — "The arts and scientific research shall be free of constraint. Academic freedom shall be respected."
Article 10 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR) — and its established jurisprudence on artistic expression, including Handyside v. United Kingdom (1976) and Vereinigung Bildender Künstler v. Austria (2007), which protect expression that may "offend, shock or disturb."
German Federal Constitutional Court jurisprudence on Kunstfreiheit — notably BVerfGE 30, 173 — Mephisto, BVerfGE 67, 213 — Anachronistischer Zug, and BVerfGE 75, 369 — Strauß-Karikatur — establishing that satirical, allegorical, performative, and fictional works must be interpreted according to their own aesthetic logic (kunsteigene Gesetzlichkeit) and not as literal factual assertion.
The Schmähkritik threshold — this work is a systemic-ontological inquiry conducted through artistic form; it does not constitute gratuitous personal insult devoid of factual or artistic engagement, and therefore remains within the sphere of constitutionally protected speech.
