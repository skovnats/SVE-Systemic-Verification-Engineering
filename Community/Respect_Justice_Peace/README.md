# [Respect · Justice · Peace](https://github.com/skovnats/SVE-Systemic-Verification-Engineering/tree/master/Community/19112025_Berlin_Bundestag_SoloPerformance/contextual_materials/mfa_public_values)
Germany’s UNSC 2027–2028 Pledge — Open Structural Justice Ledger

## This is a PROPOSAL for PUBLIC -- I do not have resources to conduct this research

> Personal, open-source archive documenting how the slogan  
> **“Respect – Justice – Peace”** is reflected (or not) in real policies and structures.

This folder is **not** a tribunal and **not** an academic institution.  
It is a quiet, long-term log created by one human being, tracking how
Germany (and, where necessary, the wider Western system it is embedded in)
relates to structural questions of justice.

The core question:

> How does the public brand **“Respect – Justice – Peace”** align with  
> long-running global arrangements in which Germany participates, benefits,
> resists, or remains silent?

The purpose is to **document**, not to condemn.  
Judgement belongs to history.

---

## 1. Scope

Each subfolder tracks one structural theme where “respect, justice, peace”
are not about single events but about **architecture**:

1. `01_cultural_heritage_restitution/`  
   – Museum collections, colonial-era loot, sacred objects, human remains.  
   – Restitution debates, returns, delays, selective transparency.

2. `02_postcolonial_monetary_systems/`  
   – CFA franc and similar currency regimes.  
   – German / EU stance, IMF/World Bank positions supported or opposed.

3. `03_resources_and_extraction/`  
   – Mining, oil, gas, rare earths, agricultural commodities.  
   – Trade rules, pricing power, export bans, sanctions, “resource security”
     strategies.

4. `04_global_supply_chains_and_labor/`  
   – Child labour, forced labour, unsafe working conditions.  
   – Supply chains for batteries, electronics, textiles, agriculture, etc.  
   – German / EU due-diligence laws and their enforcement in practice.

5. `05_military_bases_and_status_of_forces/`  
   – Foreign bases, Status of Forces agreements, long-term leased territories.  
   – Cases where local populations lost land or effective control.

6. `06_arms_exports_and_defence_industry/`  
   – Export licences, major deals, recipient regimes, end-use controversies.  
   – Positioning as a “peace actor” vs role as top weapons exporter.

7. `07_financial_architecture_and_tax_havens/`  
   – Support or tolerance for offshore structures, secrecy jurisdictions,
     profit-shifting, and extractive financial flows.  
   – German role inside EU and multilateral fora.

8. `08_maritime_routes_insurance_and_shipping/`  
   – Control over shipping lanes, naval presence, choke points.  
   – Maritime insurance, re-insurance and legal regimes shaping access.

9. `09_migration_asylum_and_externalisation/`  
   – Border regimes, pushbacks, offshoring of asylum processing.  
   – Treatment of refugees from wars where Germany is politically or
     militarily involved.

10. `10_climate_justice_and_loss_damage/`  
    – Historical emissions vs climate finance, technology transfer,
      support for vulnerable countries.  
    – Positions in UNFCCC negotiations.

11. `11_surveillance_technology_and_export_controls/`  
    – Export of interception / spyware / crowd-control technology to
      authoritarian or high-risk regimes.  
    – Oversight vs plausible deniability.

12. `12_investor_rights_vs_communities/`  
    – Investor–State Dispute Settlement (ISDS), trade and investment treaties.  
    – Cases where corporate claims conflict with social or ecological rights.

13. `13_human_rights_obligations_and_followup/`  
    – Implementation (or non-implementation) of UN/ECHR recommendations,
      treaty-body reports, and independent inquiries.

Additional themes may be added as the work evolves.

## 2. Data Structure

Each theme folder contains:

- `LOG.csv` – chronological entries.
- `sources/` – PDFs, links (mirrored when legally possible).
- `notes.md` – short, neutral clarifications where needed.

### LOG.csv — suggested columns

```text
date        – ISO date (YYYY-MM-DD)
country     – country/region primarily affected
issue       – short description ("Benin Bronzes restitution", "New CFA reform", ...)
germany_position_public
            – what was said officially (speech, press release, vote, statement)
germany_actions_concrete
            – what was actually done (or not done)
silence_or_omissions
            – relevant aspects not addressed, delayed, or avoided
structural_effect
            – how this reinforces / mitigates existing structures
sources     – URLs or local file names in ./sources
tags        – comma-separated tags (e.g. "UNSC,colonial_legacy,africa")
````

Entries should be as **neutral and factual** as possible.
Interpretation and commentary belong in separate analyses, not in this ledger.

## 3. Relation to the 444-Day Human-Rights Protocol

This archive is a **sister project** to the 444-day documentation of
human-rights violations and institutional responses in the AK1984 context.

* The 444-day protocol looks at **individual lives and concrete cases**.
* `Respect_Justice_Peace/` looks at the **global systems** that make such
  cases more or less likely.

Both archives share the same principles:

* **Transparency** – raw material and links are public.
* **Reproducibility** – anyone can re-analyse with their own methods or AIs.
* **Non-violence** – no calls to hatred or retaliation.
* **Open challenge** – anyone may contest or correct entries with evidence.

## 4. Methodology & Contributions

1. Log only what can be **sourced** (official documents, serious journalism,
   court decisions, academic work).
2. Keep language **minimal, descriptive, unemotional**.
3. If something is uncertain, mark it as such instead of speculating.
4. When Germany is one actor in a broader “Western” configuration, make that
   explicit in `issue` or `structural_effect`.

External pull requests / issues may suggest:

* corrections to factual errors,
* additional sources,
* missing key events.

All accepted changes become part of a permanent public record.

## 5. Long-Term Intention

This ledger is intended as a **slow, lifelong mirror**:

> If a country claims the mantle of “Respect, Justice, Peace”
> while seeking a UN Security Council seat,
> it is reasonable to quietly track how that claim plays out
> in museums, in money, in minerals, in seas, in borders, in weapons,
> and in the daily lives of those far from "Berlin".

No single entry is decisive.
Meaning will emerge from **decades of accumulation**.

The aim is simple:

* to ensure that beautiful words do not float unanchored,
* but remain tied to traceable decisions and their human consequences.

This repository does not judge.
It remembers.
