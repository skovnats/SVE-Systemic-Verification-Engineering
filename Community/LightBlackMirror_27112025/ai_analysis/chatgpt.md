### Short analytical explanation

**Patterns / asymmetries (bullets):**

* Most top decision-makers have **high responsibility radii (75–100)** but **low direct physical exposure** (Skin in the Game often ≤40), with a few wartime exceptions (Zelenskyy, Poroshenko, Netanyahu).
* **Children’s exposure is usually low to moderate**; many leaders are older, with adult children beyond typical draft age or living in protected environments, so the family downside is mainly political or reputational, not physical.
* **Relatives’ skin in the game spikes when leaders live in active war zones** (Ukraine, Israel) and is minimal when conflicts are fought at distance (US, EU core, China).
* **Economic vulnerability is near zero for oligarchs, major-power leaders and arms-industry CEOs**, who combine substantial personal wealth with institutional protection; this maximizes the gap between their economic risks and those of ordinary soldiers or civilians.
* **Connections to global power networks are highest (85–95)** exactly where personal exposure is lowest (arms CEOs, great-power leaders, EU executives), reinforcing insulation through diversified alliances, finance, and security.
* **Escalatory roles are often associated with low direct risk but high institutional security**, especially for leaders of nuclear or alliance powers and for arms-industry executives.
* **Where leaders face high personal and familial exposure (Ukraine’s leadership, to a degree Israel’s)**, escalation is framed as existential defense rather than discretionary policy, but still increases risk for the wider population.
* **Prior combat or hardship experience does not systematically produce restraint**: some with intense hardship (Xi, Putin, Netanyahu) adopt hard-line security policies; others with little hardship (von der Leyen, Macron) can also back escalation via alliances and sanctions.

**Summary paragraph (≤12 sentences total including bullets):**
Overall, the distribution of risk is strongly asymmetric: the people whose decisions affect millions usually face far less immediate physical and economic danger than the civilians and soldiers living in the conflict zones. The largest “incentive distance” appears where **responsibility radius and global embeddedness are high but personal exposure and economic vulnerability are low**, such as great-power leaders far from the front and executives of major defense firms. By contrast, leaders physically located in war zones with families and relatives at risk show **high skin in the game**, but their incentives can tilt toward continued escalation when survival of the state or regime is perceived as non-negotiable. Across the table, the variables that most clearly correlate with escalation are **low personal downside, strong institutional protection, and dense ties to security/industrial networks**, while the closest alignment with ordinary civilians occurs where leaders **share the same physical risks and geographic exposure** as the population they govern.
