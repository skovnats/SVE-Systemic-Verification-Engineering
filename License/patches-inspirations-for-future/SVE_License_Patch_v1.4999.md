## EUROPEAN UNION / GERMANY

### 1. eIDAS Regulation (EU) No 910/2014
**Relevance:** QES (Qualified Electronic Signature) = handwritten signature  
**Application:** S.V.E. license signed with Estonian e-Residency card = legally binding in all EU courts  
**Precedent:** Presumption of authenticity (Art. 25(2))

### 2. GDPR Article 5(1)(a) - Transparency Principle
**Relevance:** Lawful processing requires transparency  
**Application:** S.V.E. mandate for transparency aligns with EU fundamental rights  
**Case:** *Schrems II* (C-311/18) - data sovereignty, transparency imperative

### 3. German Copyright Law (UrhG) §31a
**Relevance:** Future use rights cannot be granted unknowingly  
**Application:** S.V.E. v1.4 retroactivity = clarification, not new grant  
**Principle:** Original v1.3 always required transparency

## UNITED STATES

### 4. Promissory Estoppel
**Doctrine:** One cannot deny representations relied upon by others  
**Relevance:** Entity using S.V.E. v1.3 estopped from denying v1.4 obligations  
**Case:** *Hoffman v. Red Owl Stores* (1965) - reliance creates obligation

### 5. Constructive Notice Doctrine
**Principle:** Public filing = legal notice to all  
**Application:** GitHub/IPFS publication + timestamping = constructive notice  
**Case:** *Tulsa Professional Collection v. Pope* (1988) - public records provide notice

### 6. Trade Secret Waiver
**Doctrine:** Voluntary disclosure = trade secret lost  
**Application:** Using S.V.E. = waiver of secrecy  
**Case:** *Religious Technology Center v. Netcom* (1995) - public distribution destroys secrecy claim  
**Statute:** Defend Trade Secrets Act (18 U.S.C. § 1839) - requires reasonable secrecy efforts; S.V.E. use = abandonment

### 7. Unconscionability (Contracts)
**S.V.E. Defense:** Not unconscionable because:  
- Transparency requirement disclosed upfront
- Voluntary adoption (can choose other licenses)
- Serves public interest (human dignity)
**Case:** *Williams v. Walker-Thomas Furniture* (1965) - unconscionability requires both surprise + oppression (S.V.E. has neither)

### 8. GPL Enforcement Precedent
**Case:** *Artifex Software v. Hancom* (N.D. Cal. 2017)  
**Holding:** Copyright infringement for GPL violation  
**Application:** S.V.E. share-alike = copyright condition; violation = infringement

**Case:** *Jacobsen v. Katzer* (Fed. Cir. 2008)  
**Holding:** Open source license = enforceable copyright condition  
**Application:** S.V.E. transparency = copyright condition

### 12. *Salomon v. Salomon & Co* [1897] AC 22 (UK)
**Principle:** Corporate veil piercing for fraud  
**Application:** S.V.E. δ-metrics pierce structures designed to hide influence

**Origin:** *Illinois Central Railroad v. Illinois* (1892)  
**Principle:** Resources held for public cannot be privatized without accountability  
**Application:** S.V.E. as public commons cannot be captured opaquely  
**Modern:** *National Audubon Society v. Superior Court* (1983) - public trust evolves

## ARGUMENTS S.V.E. CAN MAKE IN COURT

### Retroactivity is Valid Because:
1. **Clarification, not new obligation** (*Fairstar* precedent)
2. **Constructive notice provided** (public timestamping)
3. **Original license reserved right to evolve** (v1.3 Clause 8)
4. **Estoppel applies** (benefited from v1.3, cannot deny v1.4)

### Transparency Requirement is Enforceable Because:
1. **Copyright condition** (*Jacobsen* precedent)
2. **Voluntary adoption** (no unconscionability)
3. **Public interest** (human dignity protection)
4. **EU fundamental rights** (GDPR alignment)

### Trade Secret Waiver is Binding Because:
1. **Clear term in license** (Waiver of Secrecy clause)
2. **Public use = abandonment** (*Religious Technology Center*)
3. **Cannot claim both S.V.E. benefit AND secrecy** (doctrinal contradiction)
