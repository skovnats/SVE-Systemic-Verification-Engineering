## §1 - SCENARIO

Pharmaceutical or biotech company suppresses negative clinical trial results, including:
- Not publishing negative outcomes
- Selective reporting (publishing positive, hiding negative)
- Missing trial registry entries
- Destroying or concealing safety data
- Preventing investigators from publishing results

**Real-world pattern:** Company runs 10 trials, publishes 3 positive, hides 7 negative.

## §3 - EVIDENCE STANDARD

**Proof requires ≥2 of following:**

1. **Missing trial registry** — NCT number registered but results never published (>2 years after completion)
2. **Whistleblower testimony** — Employee/investigator reports suppression (corroborated by documents)
3. **FOIA/discovery documents** — Internal emails/memos showing concealment decision
4. **Statistical anomaly** — Published results inconsistent with known disease patterns
5. **Appendix A detection** — Semantic fingerprint shows SVE-derived methodology but no disclosure

**Burden of proof:** Preponderance of evidence (>50% probability). Not "beyond reasonable doubt."

**Company defense:** Must provide ALL trial data within 90 days of accusation. Refusal = admission of guilt.

- Patient deaths directly attributable to suppressed data

## §5 - PRECEDENT

**This case applies to:**
- All pharmaceutical companies (branded, generic, biotech)
- Medical device manufacturers (if safety data suppressed)
- Clinical research organizations (CROs hiding sponsor data)
- Academic institutions (if suppressing trial results for funding)

**Does NOT apply to:**
- Early-stage research (pre-clinical, no human trials)
- Honest mistakes (data loss due to negligence, not intent)
- Commercially sensitive formulations (if safety data fully disclosed)

**Key distinction:** Intent to conceal harm vs. negligent record-keeping.
