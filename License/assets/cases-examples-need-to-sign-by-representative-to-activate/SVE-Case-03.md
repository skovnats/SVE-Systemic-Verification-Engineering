### 1. Context (The Threat)

A healthcare or pharmaceutical entity uses S.V.E. methodologies in medical contexts — diagnostics, drug discovery, clinical trial analysis, patient risk scoring. Two dangers emerge:

**Danger A (Harm through opacity):** Entity publishes the analytical model but conceals negative clinical results, adverse effects, or dataset biases. Patients are harmed by decisions made on incomplete truth. This is Case-01 ("open code, hidden intent") applied to medicine, where the stakes are human life.

**Danger B (The Transparency Paradox in Medicine):** SVE demands radical transparency, but medical data contains patient identities, genetic information, and vulnerable health records. Naive application of "Zero Secrecy" could harm the very people SVE exists to protect.

### 2. Applicable Framework

### 3. Interpretation (The Precedent)

**3.1 · The Two Transparencies**

This case formally distinguishes between:

| Type | What is disclosed | Required? |
|---|---|---|
| **Methodological Transparency** | Algorithms, models, analytical logic, trial protocols, selection criteria, all results (positive AND negative) | **Always. No exceptions.** |
| **Personal Data Privacy** | Patient identities, individual health records, genetic sequences tied to persons | **Always protected. Disclosure = violation.** |

Zero Secrecy applies to *methods and outcomes*, never to *persons*. Protecting patient data is not secrecy — it is the Universal Benefit principle in action. Exposing patient data in the name of "transparency" is itself an SVE violation.

**3.2 · The "Hippocratic Clause"**

Any use of S.V.E. in medical, health, or pharmaceutical contexts is subject to a heightened standard: **all negative results must be published alongside positive ones.** Selective publication — showing only what supports a product, hiding what harms patients — is an Immediate Breach equivalent to Case-01 (hidden intent).

This applies to: clinical trials, diagnostic accuracy reports, adverse effect data, algorithmic bias audits on patient populations, and post-market surveillance findings.

**3.3 · Victim-First Recovery**

When SVE violation in a medical context causes patient harm, the standard recovery allocation (Case-02) is overridden:

| Recipient | Standard Allocation | Medical Harm Allocation |
|---|---|---|
| Victims (patients/families) | 30% | **60%** |
| Attorneys (per Case-02 model) | 10–30% (escalating) | 10–25% (escalating, capped) |
| SVE Foundation | 40% | 10% |
| Public good | 20% | 5% |

**Rationale:** When a person's body is harmed, money goes to that person first. The Foundation and public good still receive shares, but victims are the priority. This is non-negotiable.

Attorney fees are capped at 25% in medical cases regardless of escalation phase, to prevent legal costs from consuming what belongs to patients.

**3.4 · The Pharma-Lapse Doctrine**

If a pharmaceutical or medical entity is found to have suppressed negative S.V.E.-derived findings, and patients were harmed as a result, the following applies automatically:

1. **Immediate Breach** — no warning period, no 30-day cure.
2. **Full dataset disclosure** — all suppressed data published within 14 days.
3. **Recovery at Tier 3 minimum** — regardless of scale, the floor is Tier 3 (Severe).
4. **Regulatory referral** — SVE community formally notifies relevant health authorities.
5. **Permanent audit obligation** — entity remains under continuous SVE audit for 10 years or cessation of SVE use, whichever comes later.

### 4. Verdict

- **Finding:** Medical use of S.V.E. is permitted and encouraged, under heightened standards.
- **Key Distinctions:** Methodological transparency ≠ personal data exposure. Both are mandated — in opposite directions.
- **Recovery Override:** Victim-first allocation (60%) in all medical harm cases.
- **Severity Floor:** Suppression of medical findings = automatic Tier 3, no cure period.
