## Rationale for systems thinkers
*Optional. Skip unless you want the math behind "anti-enclosure, not just open-source."*

Copyleft (GPL, CC) protects the **syntax** of source code — **not** the underlying **ideas, ontologies, concepts**, which a closed third party can quietly enclose and carry out of the commons. SVE closes that gap: it bans enclosure of ideas/derivatives (audit-on-doubt) — a **chain of custody for the concept, not just the code.**

Survival-analysis view. Let `p` = probability any one free idea/derivative is enclosed by a closed party; once enclosed, that branch and all downstream leaves the free ecosystem. Let `wₙ ∈ [0,1]` = strategic weight of the n-th downstream derivative:

```
P(survives) ≈ wₙ · (1 − p)ⁿ
```

Since `(1 − p) < 1`, this → 0 as `n → ∞`: over generations, the strategically important ideas leak out. Under SVE, enclosure is banned, so `p = 0`:

```
P(survives) ≈ wₙ · 1ⁿ = wₙ
```

Survival is now bounded only by an idea's own usefulness (`wₙ`), not by systemic leakage. **SVE governs the dynamics, not the snapshot** — why "open now" alone isn't enough, and why Founder Mode can defer *publication* (timing) while permanently forbidding *enclosure* (ownership of ideas).

One line:  `SVE ≡ FOSS + a taboo on closing ideas/derivatives (+ audit on doubt).`

*(Argument sharpened in correspondence on the limits of copyleft; the reasoning and math are the framework author's.)*
