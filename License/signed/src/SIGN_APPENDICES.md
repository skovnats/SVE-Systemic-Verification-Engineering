## §1 — The Sacred Boundary (Personal Life is Inviolable)

**Individual and family life is sacred — SVE cannot demand personal information.**

> *"When you pray, go into your room and shut the door."* — Matthew 6:6

**Privacy zones (absolute protection):**
- Personal beliefs, thoughts, prayers
- Family relationships, marriage intimacy
- Medical history (unless directly affects job performance)
- Financial details (unless managing others' money)
- Home life and personal habits
- Children's information (always protected)
- Private communications (unless fraud is specifically suspected)

**SVE cannot demand transparency in these zones** — even with a coin flip.

**Exception:** If you voluntarily share (e.g., public testimony), that is your choice. No one can force disclosure.

## §2 — The Responsibility Threshold (When Privacy Yields to Accountability)

### Rule: Responsibility for Others = Accountability to Others

**If you manage only your own life (and/or within your family):**
- Privacy remains absolute
- Your decisions affect only you/your family → your business

**If your decisions critically affect other adults' lives:**
- Privacy in that domain yields to accountability
- Refusing transparency = grounds for demotion or removal
- Those whose lives depend on you have the right to verify trustworthiness

### What Counts as "Critical Responsibility"?

All four criteria must be met:

| Factor | Requirement | Example |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Scale** | ≥5 adults whose lives depend on your decisions | Managing 5+ employees |
| **Impact** | Decisions affect survival, safety, or major wellbeing | Budget, safety protocols, medical treatment |
| **Power asymmetry** | They cannot easily exit or override your decisions | Employees, patients, subordinates |
| **Duration** | Ongoing responsibility (not one-time) | Manager (continuous) vs. consultant (temporary) |

**Examples:**
```
✓ CEO (100 employees)      → Critical responsibility (transparent)
✓ Doctor (patients)        → Critical responsibility (transparent)
✓ Safety inspector         → Critical responsibility (transparent)
✓ Parent (minor children)  → Critical BUT family privacy applies (not SVE)
✗ Freelancer (own work)    → No critical responsibility (private)
✗ Team member (not leader) → No critical responsibility (private)
✗ Retired person           → No critical responsibility (private)
```

**Key distinction:**
- **Children:** Family privacy applies — parents accountable to God, not SVE (Deuteronomy 6:6–7)
- **Adults:** If you have power over adults → they have the right to verify you

### Types of Decisions Requiring Transparency

| Decision Type | Transparent? | Reason |
|---------------|-------------|--------|
| **Financial** (managing others' money) | ✓ Yes | Choices affect their survival |
| **Safety** (protocols, equipment) | ✓ Yes | Negligence could kill |
| **Medical** (treatment, diagnosis) | ✓ Yes | Lives depend on your accuracy |
| **Legal** (representing others) | ✓ Yes | Mistakes harm their rights |
| **Resource allocation** (who gets what) | ✓ Yes | Bias creates injustice |
| **Performance evaluation** (hire/fire) | ✓ Yes | Dishonesty ruins careers |
| **Strategic** (company direction) | Conditional | Only if affects survival |
| **Personal preferences** | ✗ No | Trivial, not critical |

**Rule:** If lying about this decision could seriously harm those depending on you → transparency required.

## §3 — Audit Mechanisms (How Transparency Works)

### What Transparency Means

**NOT:**
- Publishing home address, family photos, or private conversations
- Invasive surveillance or 24/7 monitoring
- Access to medical records unrelated to the role

**YES:**
- Integrity verification (structured interviews, polygraph where legally permitted, or equivalent — before critical decisions)
- AI audit (scan work communications for fraud patterns)
- Financial transparency (if managing others' money)
- Decision rationale (explain why you chose X)
- Conflict of interest disclosure (who benefits from your decision)

### The Choice: Accept Audit or Step Down

```
Two options only:
1. Accept transparency (integrity verification, AI audit, disclosure)
2. Decline → step down to a role with no critical responsibility

Cannot decline AND keep the position.
```

**Example:** CEO refuses integrity verification before major layoff decision → board offers advisor role (no decision power). CEO chooses: accept audit OR step down.

### Demotion Path (If Audit Refused)

1. **Refusal** → person declines transparency mechanism
2. **Assessment** → evaluate lower positions available (no critical responsibility)
3. **Offer** → propose demotion (same organization, lower role, less power)
4. **Decision** → person accepts demotion OR exits

**Coin involvement:** If unclear whether demotion is appropriate, or if multiple lower positions exist → coin decides.

**Not punishment** — demotion matches responsibility level to transparency willingness. Compensation adjusted to new role market rate (per Appendix B).

### Audit Frequency

| Role Level | Frequency | Trigger |
|------------|-----------|---------|
| **Team lead** | Quarterly | Before major decisions only |
| **Manager** | Monthly | Routine + before critical decisions |
| **Director** | Bi-weekly | Routine + spot checks |
| **VP/C-Suite** | Weekly | Routine + real-time option for critical |

**Not retroactive** — covers current and future decisions, not entire past (unless fraud is specifically suspected).

### Audit Scope

```
✓ Decision rationale (why you chose X)
✓ Conflict of interest (do you benefit personally?)
✓ Information accuracy (are facts true?)
✓ Compliance with rules (following protocols?)

✗ Personal beliefs
✗ Family life (spouse, children, home)
✗ Medical history (unless affects job performance)
✗ Personal finances unrelated to role
```

**Audit must be proportional** — no fishing expeditions.

### Integrity Verification Protocols

Integrity verification mechanisms may include, where legally permitted:

1. **Consent** — person agrees to verification or steps down (cannot force)
2. **Scope** — questions/checks limited to work decisions only
3. **Professional** — conducted by licensed examiner or certified auditor, not organization employee
4. **Recording** — full session recorded, IPFS-hashed, reviewable
5. **Interpretation** — results reviewed by independent third party
6. **Privacy** — results confidential unless fraud found

**Available mechanisms (jurisdiction-dependent):**
- Structured integrity interviews (available in all jurisdictions)
- Financial disclosure and cross-verification
- AI audit of work communications (see below)
- Polygraph testing (where legally permitted — note: inadmissible in Germany and most EU jurisdictions)

The specific mechanism used shall be the most rigorous option legally available in the applicable jurisdiction.

### AI Audit Details

**Scope:** Work communications only (email, Slack, work documents). NOT personal email, private messages, or family group chats.

**Process:**
1. AI scans → flags suspicious patterns
2. Human reviewer checks flagged items only
3. Fraud confirmed → evidence compiled; false alarm → discarded (not stored)

Cannot be used to monitor personal life, track family, or build dossiers for future use.

### Who Decides if Threshold Met?

1. **Self-assessment** → person evaluates own role
2. **Stakeholder input** → those affected vote (simple majority)
3. **Coin decision** → if disagreement → coin resolves

All audits happen on the same day, in separate rooms, with the same protocol. Prevents coordination, document destruction, and prepared/cleaned versions.
