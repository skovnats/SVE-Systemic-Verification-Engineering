00022: When someone discovers organizational harm, they climb a ladder:
00023: 
00024: | Step | Violation | Penalty Range | Example |
00025: |------|-----------|---------------|---------|
00026: | **1** | Minor delay/omission | $500K-$1M | Missed disclosure deadline by 2 weeks |
00027: | **2** | Suppression of evidence | $1M-$10M | Hid unfavorable test results |
00028: | **3** | Institutional malfeasance | $10M-$100M | Executive cover-up of safety issues |
00029: | **4** | Catastrophic harm | $100M-$1B | Concealed drug side effects → deaths |
00030: | **5** | Existential threat | $1B+ | Systemic fraud threatening public health |

00032: **Key:** 40-80% goes to informant/victims, rest to prevention programs.

00122: ## Common Misconceptions
00123: 
00124: ### "S.V.E. means no confidentiality"
00125: **False.** You can delay disclosures for legitimate reasons (legal strategy, safety, embargoes). You just must document *why* and *when* you'll disclose.
00126: 
00127: ### "Recovery Ladder penalties are bankruptcy risk"
00128: **Partially true.** Step 4-5 penalties *can* be existential — that's the point. Don't cause catastrophic harm. Steps 1-3 are proportional to reserves.
00129: 
00130: ### "Only nonprofits can adopt S.V.E."
00131: **False.** For-profit companies can use S.V.E. They just pay Commercial Tier fees and prioritize universal benefit alongside profit.
00132: 
00133: ### "It's a Christian framework"
