00037: ### 4. Promissory Estoppel
00038: **Doctrine:** One cannot deny representations relied upon by others  
00039: **Relevance:** Entity using S.V.E. v1.3 estopped from denying v1.4 obligations  
00040: **Case:** *Hoffman v. Red Owl Stores* (1965) - reliance creates obligation
00041: 
00042: ### 5. Constructive Notice Doctrine
00043: **Principle:** Public filing = legal notice to all  
00044: **Application:** GitHub/IPFS publication + timestamping = constructive notice  
00045: **Case:** *Tulsa Professional Collection v. Pope* (1988) - public records provide notice
00046: 
00047: ### 6. Trade Secret Waiver
00048: **Doctrine:** Voluntary disclosure = trade secret lost  
00049: **Application:** Using S.V.E. = waiver of secrecy  
00050: **Case:** *Religious Technology Center v. Netcom* (1995) - public distribution destroys secrecy claim  
00051: **Statute:** Defend Trade Secrets Act (18 U.S.C. § 1839) - requires reasonable secrecy efforts; S.V.E. use = abandonment
00052: 
00053: ### 7. Unconscionability (Contracts)
00054: **S.V.E. Defense:** Not unconscionable because:  
00055: - Transparency requirement disclosed upfront
00056: - Voluntary adoption (can choose other licenses)
00057: - Serves public interest (human dignity)
00058: **Case:** *Williams v. Walker-Thomas Furniture* (1965) - unconscionability requires both surprise + oppression (S.V.E. has neither)
00059: 
00060: ### 8. GPL Enforcement Precedent
00061: **Case:** *Artifex Software v. Hancom* (N.D. Cal. 2017)  
00062: **Holding:** Copyright infringement for GPL violation  
00063: **Application:** S.V.E. share-alike = copyright condition; violation = infringement
