[0032] [0033] ### 1.2 Binding Mechanisms (How S.V.E. Becomes Enforceable)
[0034] [0035] **S.V.E. binds parties through multiple overlapping mechanisms:**
[0036] [0037] #### Mechanism 1: Voluntary Adoption (Primary)
[0038] **Trigger:** Organization explicitly declares S.V.E. compliance
[0039] [0040] **Legal Effect:**
[0041] - **Promissory estoppel** - Others rely on your S.V.E. claim; you're bound
[0042] - **Good faith doctrine** - Once claimed, cannot repudiate without consequence
[0043] - **Third-party beneficiary rights** - Informants/public can enforce as intended beneficiaries
[0044] [0045] **Jurisdictional Basis:** Contract law (varies by jurisdiction; generally enforceable in Common Law and Civil Law systems)
[0046] [0047] #### Mechanism 2: Commercial Tier Agreements (Secondary)
[0048] **Trigger:** Organization enters formal S.V.E. Commercial Tier (11/22/33/44)
[0049] [0050] **Legal Effect:**
[0051] - **Explicit contract** - Written agreement with S.V.E. Organization
[0052] - **Payment obligations** - Success fees legally enforceable as debt
[0053] - **Dispute resolution** - Arbitration clauses binding
[0054] [0055] **Jurisdictional Basis:** International commercial law (New York Convention on Arbitration, UNCITRAL Model Law)
[0056] [0057] #### Mechanism 3: Public Representations (Tertiary)
[0058] **Trigger:** Organization publicly claims S.V.E. compliance (marketing, certifications, etc.)
[0059] [0060] **Legal Effect:**
[0061] - **Consumer protection laws** - False advertising if S.V.E. claim false
[0062] - **Securities fraud** (if publicly traded) - Material misrepresentation to investors
[0063] - **Defamation/libel** - Falsely claiming S.V.E. compliance harms S.V.E. Organization's reputation
[0064] [0065] **Jurisdictional Basis:** Consumer protection, securities regulation, tort law
[0066] [0067] #### Mechanism 4: Industry Standards (Emerging)
[0068] **Trigger:** S.V.E. becomes recognized professional standard in specific fields
[0069] [0070] **Legal Effect:**
[0071] - **Negligence per se** - Deviation from S.V.E. as breach of duty of care
[0072] - **Regulatory incorporation** - Governments adopt S.V.E. as compliance framework
[0073] - **Insurance requirements** - Insurers require S.V.E. compliance for coverage
[0074] [0075] **Jurisdictional Basis:** Professional standards, regulatory law, insurance contracts
[0076] [0077] ### 1.3 Severability & Savings Clauses
[0078] [0079] **If any provision of S.V.E. License is unenforceable in a jurisdiction:**
[0080] [0081] **Severability Principle:**  
[0082] Invalid provisions severed; remainder remains in force.
[0083] [0084] **However, Core Principles are Non-Severable:**
[0085] [0086] If a jurisdiction prohibits enforcement of **any Core Principle** (Section I.1, Core License v2.0), then:
[0087] - **Entire S.V.E. License is void in that jurisdiction**
[0088] - Organizations in that jurisdiction **cannot claim S.V.E. compliance**
[0089] - Operations must relocate to friendly jurisdiction or cease S.V.E. use
[0090] [0091] **Rationale:** Core principles are interdependent. Removing one (e.g., "transparency") while keeping others (e.g., "universal benefit") creates incoherent system.
[0092] [0093] **Example:**
[0094] - **Jurisdiction A** bans transparency for "national security" → S.V.E. cannot operate there
[0095] - **Jurisdiction B** permits everything except specific penalty amounts → Amounts adjusted; rest of S.V.E. valid
